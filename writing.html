OPINION ESSAY INTRODUCTION · EXAM PREPARATION

🎯 Learn the method — practise the assessment criteria

In the writing assessment, you will produce a 100–120-word introduction. Your introduction should set the context, introduce the debate, state a clear position and outline the main arguments. Use the five assessment criteria as a guide to improve your writing, rather than focusing on a mark. The practice topics below are different from the assessment topic.

1. ContextIntroduce the issue and why it matters.
2. DebateShow the competing perspectives fairly.
3. ThesisState your position precisely.
4. DirectionBriefly signal the arguments to follow.

🧪 Introduction workshop

Practice topic: Should universities make financial literacy a compulsory subject?

Write an introduction to an opinion essay. Present the issue, both sides, your position and the direction of your argument. The workshop helps you respect the format, but it does not automatically grade your free writing.

Show an example after writing

Financial decisions increasingly shape young adults’ lives, from managing monthly expenses to understanding student loans. Some people therefore believe that universities should provide compulsory financial literacy lessons, while others argue that degree programmes are already overcrowded and that students should be free to choose. Although additional teaching would require time and resources, I believe a short, practical course would benefit most students. It could help them avoid common financial mistakes and make more informed decisions at the beginning of their careers. This essay will first consider the value of these skills before examining how universities could introduce the subject without placing an excessive burden on their existing programmes.

YOUR CEFR LEARNING JOURNEY

🌱 From getting started to confident argumentation

These levels describe writing skills you can practise. They are not grades or scores. This learning path takes you progressively from A2 to C1, using the five assessment criteria.

A2 · Building sentences

Introduce a topic and give a simple opinion using familiar vocabulary and basic links.

B1 · Connecting ideas

Present the issue, acknowledge the debate and write a connected paragraph.

B2 · Developing an argument

Present both sides, a clear thesis and the arguments to follow, using varied and generally accurate English.

C1 · Refining your position

Frame the tension thoughtfully, use precise vocabulary and distinguish evidence from uncertainty.

Choose a task at a level that helps you progress. You can revisit any activity as often as you like.
