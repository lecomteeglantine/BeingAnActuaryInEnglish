Who Gets the Medicine? · The Global Access Challenge

Work in teams of 3–4. Negotiate access to a life-saving medicine, balance competing priorities and build a structured team pitch of no more than 2:00 in total for the whole group — not 2 minutes per student.

Your final output

One prepared team pitch · 2:00 total for the whole group

Important: the whole team shares one 2:00 limit — it is not 2 minutes per student. This is not an improvised recap at the end: every confirmed decision adds material to your Pitch Builder. After each consequence, agree on one sentence you could say aloud.

2:00HARD STOP
🎯1 · Challenge & priorityabout 20 sec

What is the access problem? Who or what did you prioritise?

🧩2 · Your agreementabout 35 sec

Explain allocation, pricing and licensing choices.

⚖️3 · Main trade-offabout 35 sec

Connect royalties and manufacturing to one difficult compromise.

📈4 · Response & monitoringabout 20 sec

Explain your emergency response and what you would monitor next.

Whole-team time rule: aim for roughly 1:45–1:55. All speakers and all four parts must fit inside the same 2:00 total; the final seconds are your safety buffer.

Student instructions

  1. Form a team of 3 or 4 and assign roles. Every student must contribute and speak, but the entire group shares one 2:00 pitch.
  2. Read the scenario and country cards. Identify the access problem, the shortage and the main constraints.
  3. Make 6 decisions together. For each one, discuss all five options before confirming one shared choice. Only confirmed choices are saved on this device.
  4. After every consequence, stop for a Pitch Checkpoint. Agree on one short sentence: what you chose, why, and the trade-off. Your Pitch Builder updates automatically. After Decisions 1, 3 and 5, complete the illustrated Flash Mission too: it takes about 30 seconds and gives you useful evidence or wording for the pitch.
  5. Do not chase a “perfect” score. Your job is to build a defensible agreement and explain its compromises.
  6. Rehearse your final team pitch. Follow the four-part structure above, use at least four Session 1 terms, make sure everyone speaks, and stop before 2:00 for the whole group.

Useful language

Use careful, professional English.

Explain a choice

“We chose this option because…”
“Our main priority is…”
“This would improve access to medicines by…”

Balance arguments

“On the one hand…, on the other hand…”
“This is more equitable, but…”
“The trade-off is that…”

Handle uncertainty

“This is likely to…”
“This may be difficult because…”
“We cannot assume that…”

Conclude the pitch

“Our final agreement is based on…”
“On balance, we recommend…”
“We would monitor… to assess impact.”
