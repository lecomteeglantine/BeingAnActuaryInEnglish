🌊 Risk Exposure

Understand where risk is higher and why. After your worksheet, use this short revision space to practise describing maps accurately and comparing locations in English.

← Back to the 16-session programme

Three essentials from your worksheet

🗺️

1. Read the legend first

Map colours do not always mean the same thing. Red may mark buildings, not the highest hazard. Describe only what the map actually shows.

“The red shapes represent buildings. We need the flood-hazard legend before drawing conclusions.”
📍

2. Say where things are

Use near, within, along, across, through, far from and on to locate buildings, rivers and risk zones.

“Several properties are near the river.”
“The mapped flood zone extends along the coast.”
⚖️

3. Compare carefully

Make accurate comparatives: higher than, closer to … than, more exposed than, riskier than. Qualify a conclusion when the evidence is incomplete.

“Zone A appears more exposed than Zone B, but we need comparable hazard data.”

Quick self-check · 3 questions

Try these before the team mission. Select one answer per question to see feedback. No scores or accounts are saved.

1. A map shows red building footprints. What can you conclude?

2. Zone A is 200 metres from a river. Zone B is 800 metres away. Which comparison is correct?

3. You are comparing two maps that use different colour scales. What should you do?
