Pronunciation review

Actuarial English is full of long technical words, percentages and numbers. The goal is not an accent: it is to make the analysis easy to follow.

Word stress

Stress the right syllable in long technical words.

  • /ˈæk.tʃu.ə.ri/
  • /ˌæk.tʃuˈeə.ri.əl/
  • /ˌprɒb.əˈbɪl.ə.ti/
  • /ˌkɒr.əˈleɪ.ʃən/

Numbers & percentages

Chunk long numbers and make the key contrast audible.

  • vs

Stress the contrasting information: “The loss ratio rose from sixty-EIGHT to seventy-FOUR per cent.”

Final -s

Plural and third-person endings can sound /s/, /z/ or /ɪz/.

  • /s/
  • /z/
  • /ɪz/

-ed endings

Past forms use /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/.

  • /t/
  • /d/
  • /ɪd/

Sentence stress

Stress the information that carries the message, not every word.

Think: MAIN DRIVER · SHARP INCREASE · CLAIM SEVERITY.

Presenting uncertainty

Intonation can show confidence or caution.

Slow down before the caveat. Do not rush the sentence ending.
